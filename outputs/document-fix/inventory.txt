139	[]	INTRODUCTION
140	[]	Project Context
141	[]	          Effective administrative service delivery is essential to maintaining service quality and institutional efficiency in educational institutions. As the volume of client transactions increases, administrative offices must process requests while maintaining an orderly, transparent, and fair service flow. Manual queuing procedures can contribute to long waiting times, congestion, unclear service order, and difficulty in monitoring service performance.
142	[]	          At Lyceum of Alabang, the Registrar and Finance Offices handle transactions that require clients to wait for available service personnel. To improve this process, the proposed system establishes structured Regular and Priority ticket workflows. Regular tickets follow the standard queue sequence, while Priority tickets are intended for verified qualified clients, including Persons with Disabilities (PWDs), senior citizens, pregnant women, and other eligible individuals. 
143	[]	          To address these issues,  This study proposes to development of an Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring in Lyceum of Alabang. The system uses a kiosk to support client identification or guest processing, department and transaction selection, Regular or Priority ticket selection, and queue-ticket generation. A digital display board presents queue information and supports audio-visual announcements so that clients can monitor the progress of service without relying solely on a physical line. Priority processing includes a controlled security authorization process. Designated security personnel verify qualified clients and authorize Priority ticket requests through a PIN-protected interface. This mechanism helps prevent unauthorized use of priority processing while maintaining a clear distinction between Regular and Priority tickets.
144	[]	          The system supports Registrar, Cashier, ITM, Security Personnel, Administrator, and Client functions. Registrar and Cashier staff manage their assigned queues and service windows, while ITM staff serve as backup personnel when Cashier or Registrar staff are unavailable. As backup staff, ITM personnel can perform the necessary staff functions of the unavailable office and process its Regular and Priority tickets. Staff can call the next eligible ticket, complete transactions, and mark tickets as skipped or no-show. The system also supports service-window status and assignment information that is synchronized with the centralized database. For administrators, the system provides a dashboard, queue monitoring, analytics, reports, staff management, display-board settings, and activity logs. Service-performance information includes queue volume, waiting time, service time, completion rate, turnaround time, peak periods, staff performance, and service-window performance. These functions allow queue activity to be monitored as operational data and help administrators evaluate service delivery using recorded system transactions.
145	[]	The system also supports four departments, namely Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. It provides a Main Admin account that oversees overall system operations and four separate Department Head Admin accounts, each assigned to a specific department. To balance queue processing, the system implements a 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence, helping prevent excessive delays among Regular clients. Furthermore, the system integrates Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk to support administrative monitoring and decision-making.
146	[]	
147	[]	Purpose and Description
148	[]	          The purpose of this study is to design and develop an Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring that improves administrative queuing at Lyceum of Alabang. The system supports Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission through automated Regular and Priority ticket processing, department-based administration, and intelligent queue monitoring to promote organized and measurable service operations.
149	[]	          The kiosk serves as the primary client-facing interface. Clients enter the required information or proceed as guests, select the appropriate department and transaction, and choose the applicable Regular or Priority workflow. Priority requests require verification and PIN-based authorization by designated security personnel before a Priority ticket is generated. The system then produces a queue ticket containing the information required for service routing and queue tracking.
150	[]	The system supports Administrator, Staff, Security Personnel, and Client functions. 
151	[]	          Administrators manage monitoring, analytics, reports, staff accounts, display settings, service-window information, and activity records. Registrar and Cashier staff process Regular and Priority queue transactions assigned to their departments, while ITM staff serve as backup personnel when Cashier or Registrar staff are unavailable and can perform the necessary staff functions of the unavailable office. Security personnel verify eligibility for Priority processing, and clients follow queue progress through the digital display board and audio announcements. By storing queue records, assignments, statuses, and service timestamps in a centralized database, the system provides consistent information across supported interfaces. The analytics and reporting modules use these records to summarize queue activity and service-performance indicators for selected periods and departments.
152	[]	The Main Admin manages and monitors the overall system, while the four Department Head Admins have similar administrative functions restricted to their assigned departments. The system also incorporates a 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence and three intelligent behaviors that provide waiting-time estimates, peak-hour forecasts, and queue congestion risk assessments.

185	[]	Scope and Limitation
186	[]	          This study focuses on the development of an Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring for four departments of Lyceum of Alabang: Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. The system supports kiosk-based Regular and Priority ticket generation, security authorization, staff queue processing, and a 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence. It also provides a Main Admin account for overall system management, four Department Head Admin accounts for department-specific administration, and three intelligent behaviors for queue monitoring and operational assessment.
187	[]	          Within the defined scope, the system supports client identification or guest processing, department and transaction selection, Regular and Priority queue-ticket generation, Registrar and Cashier queue handling, ITM backup staff access when Cashier or Registrar staff are unavailable, staff operations, service-window management, queue monitoring, audio-visual announcements, a configurable digital display board, administrative monitoring, analytics, reports, staff management, and activity logging. The system stores queue records, service statuses, staff and service-window assignments, and relevant timestamps in a centralized MySQL database. These records support synchronized information across the kiosk, staff interfaces, display board, and administrative modules and provide the basis for supported service-performance calculations.
188	['p1']	          The system is limited to the configured offices, service windows, users, transactions, and ITM function included in the capstone implementation. It is designed for internal institutional queue management and does not provide external online queue reservations, remote appointment booking, or integration with institutional systems outside the defined project scope. The intelligent behaviors depend on available queue records and historical service data. Predictions and congestion assessments may vary depending on queue conditions and the amount of recorded information. Department Head Admin accounts are also restricted to their assigned departments, while the Main Admin maintains overall administrative access. Performance measures depend on valid records captured by the system; information that is not recorded by the system is not assumed or reconstructed.

330	[]	The reviewed literature and related systems show that automated queue management systems help improve services and reduce for a long waiting time in different organizations. Many studies explain that changing manual queuing into digital systems makes the process even faster, more organized and easier for the both staff and users.
331	[]	Local studies such as the Automated Enrolment Queueing System, GS Services Digital Queuing System, and Hospital Queuing System with Smart Kiosk show that automatic ticket issuance the digital display boards and kiosks to help reduce crowding and confusion in service areas. These systems show that having a proper queue system helps manage many clients at the same time and improves the overall service experience.
332	[]	International studies also support these ideas by adding technologies like cloud systems, mobile applications, and monitoring tools. Systems like Smart Queue Management System, NoQ Real-Time Queue Software, and GiFa Intelligent Queue Manager use real-time updates and notifications to make queue handling more fair and organized. However, most of these systems focus only on managing queues and basic monitoring. Only few systems include performance tracking and KPI monitoring to measure how well the service is performing.
333	[]	Because of this the proposed Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring for the Lyceum of Alabang plans to combine kiosk ticketing, automated queue handling, audio-visual announcements, and performance monitoring into one system. This will help the organization manage queues better and also monitor service performance for improvement. 
334	[]	The enhanced system extends the basic queuing functions discussed in the existing literature by incorporating service-performance analytics, KPI monitoring, detailed reporting, configurable queue displays, service-window management, staff management, activity monitoring, and real-time synchronization. The developed system further extends these capabilities by supporting Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission through department-based queue management. It implements a 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence and provides separate Main Admin and Department Head Admin access. Additionally, Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk strengthen the system’s ability to assess queue conditions and support administrative decision-making. The additional functions allow queue information to be examined not only as a list of waiting customers but also as operational data that can be used to evaluate service performance. Service-window performance is derived from recorded service timestamps and actual system assignments rather than unsupported assumptions, helping maintain more reliable performance information.

455	['ListParagraph']	Idle Time
456	[]	Idle time refers to the duration when a service window is not actively serving any client. Monitoring idle time helps identify inefficiencies in staff utilization and service distribution. Idle Time is only considered measurable when reliable historical operating-session intervals are available. A closed service window is not automatically treated as an idle window. For this reason, utilization is determined only when sufficient operating-time information is available. Active Time is based on valid completed service timestamps, while invalid timestamps and waiting, serving, cancelled, skipped, or no-show records are excluded from completed-service calculations.
457	['ListParagraph']	Processing Time (Duration)
458	[]	Processing time refers to the amount of time required to complete a single transaction. This metric is used to evaluate the efficiency of service delivery for both Registrar and Cashier operations.
459	['ListParagraph']	System Capacity (Throughput)
460	[]	System capacity, also known as throughput, refers to the number of clients served within a specific period (e.g., per hour). This metric measures the overall performance and capability of the system in handling large volumes of transactions.
461	['ListParagraph']	Comparison Between Priority and Regular Processes
462	[]	The study compares the performance of priority and regular queues in terms of waiting time, processing time, and service efficiency. This comparison helps determine whether priority handling improves service delivery without negatively affecting regular clients.
463	['ListParagraph']	Results Based on Ratios and Volume
464	[]	System performance is also analyzed using ratios and volume-based data, such as the number of clients served per hour, the ratio of priority to regular clients, and the percentage improvement in waiting time. These measurements provide a quantitative basis for evaluating system effectiveness.

702	[]	Project Description
703	[]	The Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring in Lyceum of  Alabang was developed to provide a more organized and efficient way of managing queues within the school. The system handles the queuing process of four departments, namely Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. Through the kiosk, clients can select the service they need and receive either a Regular or Priority queue ticket. 
704	[]	To balance the serving of clients, the system follows a 4 Priority to 2 Regular queue sequence. This means that four Priority clients can be served followed by two Regular clients. This setup was added so that Regular clients who have been waiting earlier will not be continuously left behind when there are many Priority clients in the queue. 
705	[]	The system also has different levels of administrative access. Aside from the Main Admin, four separate Department Head Admin accounts are provided for Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. These accounts have administrative functions similar to the Main Admin, but their access is limited to their assigned department. This allows each department head to focus on the queue activities and performance of their own department, while the Main Admin can monitor the overall system. 
706	[]	One of the main features of the system is its System Intelligence, which consists of three intelligent behaviors: Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk. These features use current and previously recorded queue information to provide useful estimates and assessments that can help the Main Admin and Department Head Admins monitor their operations. 
707	[]	The Predicted Waiting Time estimates how long a client may need to wait before being served. It considers information such as the current queue, available service windows, and recent service performance. The Peak Hour Prediction uses historical queue arrivals and recent queue activity to estimate the busiest upcoming time of a department. Lastly, the Smart Queue Congestion Risk evaluates the current condition of the queue by considering factors such as queue length, arrival rate, service rate, active windows, and queue trends. It provides a congestion risk assessment together with a reason and recommended action.
708	['NormalWeb']	Together with these intelligent behaviors, the system provides queue monitoring, analytics, KPI monitoring, reporting, queue display, ticket printing, and department-based administration. These features allow the system to not only manage clients in the queue but also provide information that can help administrators monitor service conditions and make better operational decisions.

These include the Kiosk Module, Queue Management Module, Staff Module, Queue Display Module, Main Admin Module, Department Head Admin Module, Analytics and KPI Monitoring, Reports, and the system's intelligent behaviors. 
711	['NormalWeb']	The Kiosk Module is mainly used by clients. It allows them to select the department or transaction they need and get either a Regular or Priority queue ticket. Once a ticket is generated, the information is recorded in the database and added to the appropriate queue.

This means that after four Priority clients, two Regular clients are included in the serving order. This setup helps prevent Regular clients from waiting for too long when several Priority clients enter the queue.

755	[]	CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
756	[]	Summary of Findings
757	[]	This study focused on the development of an Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring in Lyceum of Alabang. The system was developed to improve the existing queuing process by providing automated ticket generation, organized queue management, real-time monitoring, and service performance evaluation. It was designed to support four departments, namely Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission, while providing a more organized and convenient queuing experience for clients and staff.
758	[]	The developed system provides a kiosk-based interface that allows clients to select their department, choose their transaction, and generate Regular or Priority queue tickets. The system stores queue information in a centralized database, allowing staff to monitor and process transactions according to their assigned departments. The digital queue display board and audio announcements also provide clients with updated information about the tickets currently being served and their assigned service windows. These features support a more systematic approach to managing queue transactions and reduce the need for manual queue handling.
759	[]	To maintain a balanced serving process, the system implements a 4 Priority to 2 Regular queue sequence. Under this arrangement, four Priority clients are served followed by two Regular clients when both queue classifications have waiting clients. This feature is designed to prevent Regular clients from being continuously delayed during periods of high Priority queue volume while maintaining appropriate accommodation for qualified Priority clients. The serving arrangement provides a structured approach to managing both queue classifications within the supported departments.
760	[]	The system also provides different levels of administrative access through the Main Admin and four separate Department Head Admin accounts. The Main Admin is responsible for monitoring and managing the overall system, including queue activities, staff information, analytics, reports, and performance across all four departments. Meanwhile, each Department Head Admin is assigned to one specific department, namely Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, or Admission. These accounts provide administrative functions similar to those of the Main Admin, but their access is restricted to the information and operations of their assigned departments. This structure supports centralized supervision while allowing each department to manage and monitor its own queue activities.
761	[]	Furthermore, the system integrates three intelligent behaviors consisting of Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk. Predicted Waiting Time estimates how long clients may need to wait based on the current queue, active service windows, and recent service performance. Peak Hour Prediction uses historical queue records and recent activities to identify possible busy periods. Smart Queue Congestion Risk evaluates factors such as queue length, arrival rate, service rate, available windows, and queue trends to assess congestion conditions and provide recommended actions. These features provide the Main Admin with an overall view of the four departments, while Department Head Admins can access intelligence information relevant to their assigned departments. The resulting estimates and assessments help support informed operational decisions, although their reliability depends on the availability of sufficient and accurate queue data.
762	[]	The system provides Service Performance Analytics and Key Performance Indicator (KPI) Monitoring to evaluate recorded queue activities and service operations. These functions present information such as queue volume, waiting time, service time, completed transactions, peak periods, and service-window performance. Through these features, administrators can review the performance of different departments and identify areas that may require improvement. The integration of analytics and KPI monitoring allows the system to provide not only automated queue processing but also measurable information that supports service performance evaluation.
763	[]	The system underwent Alpha Testing and Beta Testing to assess its functionality, usability, reliability, and overall performance. Based on the evaluation results presented in Chapter 4, the clients’ overall weighted mean was 4.25, interpreted as Very Good, while the IT experts’ overall weighted mean was reported as 4.35, also interpreted as Very Good. The consolidated evaluation produced an overall weighted mean of 4.30, interpreted as Very Good. Among the evaluated characteristics, Performance Efficiency received a weighted mean of 4.70, Security received 4.62, and Reliability received 4.57, all interpreted as Excellent. These results indicate that the system received favorable evaluations based on the criteria used in the study.
764	[]	The findings indicate that the Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring provides an integrated approach to automated queue processing, department-based administration, intelligent queue monitoring, and service performance evaluation. The inclusion of four departments, separate administrative access, a balanced Regular and Priority serving sequence, and three intelligent behaviors supports the system’s objective of providing a more organized, transparent, and data-driven queuing process for Lyceum of Alabang.
765	[]	
766	[]	Conclusions
767	[]	Based on the findings of the study, the researchers concluded that the Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring provides a practical solution for improving the organization and management of administrative queues at Lyceum of Alabang. By integrating kiosk-based ticket generation, automated queue processing, digital queue displays, and centralized monitoring, the system provides an alternative to manual queuing procedures and supports a more structured service process.
768	[]	The implementation of the 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence establishes a defined approach to balancing Priority and Regular transactions. This arrangement is intended to maintain appropriate service consideration for qualified Priority clients while helping prevent Regular clients from being continuously delayed when Priority demand increases.
769	[]	The inclusion of Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission expands the system’s coverage across four school departments. Furthermore, the separation between the Main Admin and Department Head Admin accounts establishes clear administrative responsibilities. The Main Admin maintains overall control and monitoring of the system, while each Department Head Admin is limited to managing and reviewing information relevant to the assigned department. This provides a more organized approach to administrative access and department-level supervision.
770	[]	The integration of Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk also strengthens the system’s monitoring capabilities. These intelligent behaviors provide estimates, forecasts, and risk assessments that may help administrators identify possible service delays, anticipate busy periods, and assess queue congestion. However, the accuracy and usefulness of these features depend on the availability and quality of recorded queue and historical data.
771	[]	Moreover, the system’s analytics and KPI monitoring features provide administrators with measurable information about queue activities and service performance. The recorded information can support the identification of operational concerns, monitoring of service efficiency, and assessment of areas that may require improvement.
772	[]	The overall evaluation result of 4.30, interpreted as Very Good, indicates that the system received a favorable assessment based on the evaluation criteria used in the study. The results support the suitability of the system for its intended queuing functions, although continued testing and evaluation are necessary to establish its effectiveness under different operating conditions.
773	[]	Therefore, the researchers concluded that the developed Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring can support a more organized, transparent, and data-driven approach to managing queues at Lyceum of Alabang. Its combination of automated queue processing, department-based administration, predictive information, and service performance monitoring provides a foundation for further improvements in the institution’s administrative service delivery.

893	[]	ABSTRACT
894	[]	This study developed an Intelligent Queuing Management System with Integrated Service Performance Analytics and KPI Monitoring for Lyceum of Alabang to address manual queue handling, waiting times, and congestion. Using Agile methodology, the researchers built kiosk-based Regular and Priority ticketing, a digital queue display, administrative monitoring, analytics, and reporting for Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. A 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence aims to prevent prolonged Regular queue delays. The system includes a Main Admin for overall operations and four Department Head Admins with department-specific access. Its three intelligent behaviors Predicted Waiting Time, Peak Hour Prediction, and Smart Queue Congestion Risk provide operational estimates and assessments. Alpha and Beta Testing were conducted, and Chapter 4 reports an overall evaluation weighted mean of 4.30, interpreted as Very Good. The system offers a structured, data-supported approach to queue management, although predictive accuracy requires further validation.

896	[]	INTRODUCTION
897	[]	Administrative offices in educational institutions handle enrollment, payments, document requests, and other services. Manual queuing can lead to long waits, congestion, unclear service order, and limited performance monitoring. These concerns create a need for automated ticket processing and reliable service information.
898	[]	The researchers developed an Intelligent Queuing Management System for Lyceum of Alabang that combines kiosk ticketing, Regular and Priority queue processing, staff panels, live queue displays, and administrative analytics. The system aims to support orderly service delivery and measurable performance monitoring.
899	[]	The system supports Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission. Its 4 Priority to 2 Regular serving sequence is intended to accommodate qualified Priority clients while ensuring Regular clients continue to be served. A Main Admin oversees the entire system; four Department Head Admins have access restricted to their respective departments.
900	[]	Three intelligent behaviors provide decision support: Predicted Waiting Time estimates queue delays; Peak Hour Prediction forecasts busy periods using historical arrivals; and Smart Queue Congestion Risk assesses queue length, arrival and service rates, active windows, and queue trends. This study developed and evaluated these functions alongside analytics and KPI monitoring.
901	[]	
902	[]	METHODS
903	[]	Research and Development Approach
904	[]	The study used Agile methodology, covering requirements gathering, system design, iterative development, testing, and improvement. Observations, interviews, and document analysis informed requirements for queue sequencing, Priority authorization, and service operations.
905	[]	System design included interface prototypes, database relationships, and diagrams such as UML, DFD, and ERD. Development iterations implemented the kiosk, staff panels, display board, admin features, and intelligence functions.
906	[]	System Development and Architecture
907	[]	The front end was developed in React, the back end in PHP, and the centralized database in MySQL. A Mini PC powers the kiosk and connects to a touchscreen and thermal printer. Staff computers, admin interfaces, and the queue display communicate over a local area network.
908	[]	The kiosk generates Regular or security-authorized Priority tickets. Staff can call, complete, or skip tickets, while the display board shows serving numbers and window assignments. The administrative modules offer department-filtered monitoring, reporting, analytics, and intelligent assessments.
909	[]	Testing and Evaluation
910	[]	Alpha Testing checked functional behavior and identified defects during development. Beta Testing gathered feedback from selected users on functionality, usability, and reliability.
911	[]	
912	[]	The methodology lists nineteen purposively selected respondents: fifteen clients, one Registrar staff member, one Cashier staff member, one security guard, and one IT expert. Evaluation procedures describe open-ended interviews and Chapter 4 reports software-quality weighted means.

915	[]	Integrated Queuing Operations
916	[]	The system integrates kiosk ticket generation, staff queue processing, printed tickets, digital announcements, monitoring, and database-backed records for Accounting/Cashier, Registrar, ITM, and Admission.
917	[]	A 4 Priority to 2 Regular sequence structures service order when both queues have waiting clients. The Main Admin handles overall administration, while the four Department Head Admins can access only their assigned departments.
918	[]	
919	[]	System Intelligence and Performance Monitoring
920	[]	Predicted Waiting Time estimates likely delays from current queues, available windows, and service history. Peak Hour Prediction uses historical arrivals to identify busy periods, but may be unavailable with insufficient data. Smart Queue Congestion Risk assesses queue length, arrival rate, service rate, active windows, and queue trends, then presents a risk level and suggested action.
921	[]	Analytics and KPI monitoring summarize ticket volume, waiting and service times, completed transactions, peak periods, and window performance to support administrative review.

929	[]	DISCUSSION
930	[]	The developed system integrates queue ticketing with department-based monitoring, analytics, and predictive assessments. A centralized database helps synchronize kiosk, staff, display, and administrative information.
931	[]	The 4 Priority to 2 Regular sequence seeks to balance service priority and Regular client access, but actual waiting-time improvements need further measurement during busy periods.
932	[]	Four-department coverage and restricted Department Head Admin access support local oversight while preserving overall supervision by the Main Admin.
933	[]	The intelligence features provide estimates and risk information rather than guaranteed outcomes. In particular, peak-hour forecasts depend on sufficient historical data, and predicted waits can change with queue conditions.

936	[]	CONCLUSIONS
937	[]	The researchers concluded that the system provides a functional approach to automated queue processing and performance monitoring at Lyceum of Alabang. It combines kiosk ticketing, queue displays, administration, and analytics in one platform.
938	[]	Four-department support, the 4 Priority to 2 Regular sequence, and separate Main Admin and Department Head Admin access establish defined operational processes. The three intelligence features supplement monitoring with estimates and risk assessments.

940	[]	RECOMMENDATIONS
